Join policy (the main session's call, recorded by the leader, 2026-10-22): no join attempts until CR1's capture lands and the crash is at least characterised. A join's ring has become a coin flip, and a lucky green proves nothing about the change. Each held track joins after its review plus one green ring run with the capture wrapper on, so a crash during that ring yields the dump the investigation needs instead of a rerun.

Policy replaced (leader decision on the main session's question, 2026-10-22), option (b) with a rule: crash loop C ran 12 of 12 wrapped full runs green with 0 dumps, against 1 crash in 8 unwrapped runs, so the attached debugger very likely hides the fault (Inferred, consistent with a start-up timing race). A green ring under the wrapper therefore proves no more than the join's own ring, and the hold would wait on a capture that may never come. Held tracks now join on their reviews plus the join's own full ring, under this rule: 1. A Desktop child that exits with a negative code (the harness names it a native crash) is not a verdict. Keep its Desktop log, then rerun that mode, all its parts, alone. Green: the join proceeds, and the instance is recorded (mode, part, the last line printed, load at the time). 2. If the rerun crashes too, rerun once more. Two consecutive crashes in the same mode, or any crash after the crashed part had printed a check, stop the join: that no longer matches the known start-up pattern and may be the change. 3. Any non-crash red is a real red. 3a. Timeouts (exit 124), added 2026-10-22 after loop E: under two or more concurrent harness parents, children hit the 120 s timeout in several modes. At a join, a 124 is handled like a crash: rerun that mode, all parts, alone with nothing else running; green, the join proceeds and the instance is recorded. A 124 on that quiet rerun is a real red (a hang the change may have introduced). Joins run with the investigation's loops paused, so the ring is not loaded by them. 4. CR1's wrapper stays an opt-in capture; loop D (two wrapped runners at once) and, if it stays clean, CR2 (an in-process vectored exception handler writing a minidump on 0xC0000374) continue in parallel as the investigation.

The dump (main session, Verified from a minidump read with its own readers and ntdll symbols): exception 0xC0000374 on a native thread with no managed frames, raised by the segment heap's garbage-collection timer (RtlpHpGCTimerCallback → … → RtlReportFatalFailure), heap failure type 17 (VS subsegment corruption). The bytes before the corrupted block header are 12-byte (x, y, ARGB) vertex records in pixel range, running over the block's tail guard: a heap buffer overrun by a writer of a per-vertex-colour vertex stream, detected later by a timer on a thread-pool thread, which explains why it is intermittent, hits any mode, and names no culprit. Inferred: the writer is in the rendering stack (Skia Ganesh or ANGLE), not our managed code; no P/Invoke of ours writes such a stream.

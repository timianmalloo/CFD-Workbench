Rail comb

Proposal for OI-5, revised after Ruling 194 · mockup for approval before any build · planform rails, 900 mm half-span example

Checking…

1 · The Plan view, live

Move the pointer over the wing, or focus the plan and press [ and ] to walk the points (the Tracing strip then reads "Point N:"). The Tracing strip beneath gives the radius of both rails at the pointer's station; rings mark where. Use the plate top right: Smaller or Larger teeth, Auto, Sparser, Denser. At a limit a stepper keeps focus and says so. The harness above switches planform, area (CAD or Analysis), the selected point, the comb, the narrow width and the theme; its probe slider is a harness stand-in for the pointer and is not part of the product. In the product the arrow keys nudge the focused point, so they are not a probe. Scale and density are view settings: the foil is untouched and nothing enters undo.

2 · One planform at two scales

Same rails, same instant. Top: Auto (the 90th-percentile tooth is 30 px). Bottom: a fixed step, 30 px = 0.5 per m. At the larger gain the sharp tip teeth run past 60 px and are clipped and marked ×; the plate counts them. The legend line states what a tooth means, so two screenshots can be compared. A thin line joins the tooth tips of each continuous piece of the rail.

3 · The monotone-piece count

A reading, not a grade. A numbered tick sits on the rail at each boundary between pieces of the curvature plot (a peak or a dip of curvature, not an inflection), and the plate names each one in text ("LE1 at η 0.71"). Left: the example planform. Right: one trailing-edge control point moved so the curvature wobbles. Both are legal foils. The count says where to look.

4 · Hard states

Each card is a state the build must render. Views are cropped to the region that matters at 1 px per mm.

5 · Density

Teeth per rail, spaced evenly by arc length over the whole rail, with the tip region of the leading edge at 16 and at 128. Break teeth at corners and at curvature jumps are always drawn. Teeth closer than 3 px are thinned.

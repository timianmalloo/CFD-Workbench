1 · The drag at the limit: today and two alternatives

What to compare. Same gesture in each column: the pointer drags the trailing-edge tip vertex toward the leading edge, down to a tip chord of 2 mm. The rule is max(5 mm, 2 % of the root chord), so the limit here is mm. The drawing is the outer 60 mm of the half-wing at 6 px per mm. Today is column C. Columns A and B are the choice in DR-LIM-1.

2 · Widening the root: the message says what to do

Same rule, other end. The Example foil's tip is 6 mm. A root chord above mm would put the tip under 2 % of it. Today the refusal names the tip, which the designer did not touch. The proposal names the root, gives the limit, and says what lifts it.

3 · Keyboard: arrows hold at the limit, one announcement

Shift+Down moves the tip vertex 1 mm per press (the channel ladder: 0.01 · 0.1 · 1 mm for Command, plain, Shift). Tip chord starts at 8 mm. Presses past the limit do not bank steps, so one Up press moves it back at once. The strip is a live region and speaks once, on arrival at the limit. Key-up ends the run as one undo step.

Choices for the operator

  1. DR-LIM-1 drag at the limit. A hold (recommended) · B drag freely, release lands at the limit · C keep today. A matches how a constrained sketch point behaves in Fusion, SolidWorks and SolveSpace (recalled, not cited), and keeps every frame legal so the Wing block never shows a wing the product would refuse. B shows an illegal wing during the drag and surprises at release. C loses the gesture.
  2. DR-LIM-2 root drag holds too (recommended) or refuses at release.
  3. DR-LIM-3 copy. COPY-B to COPY-F as drawn here; COPY-A is Ruling 94.
  4. DR-LIM-4 spec line under CAD-04 and the tip-chord paragraph.
  5. DR-LIM-5 order. This now, then group move and typed value for several points (node M), or the reverse.
  6. DR-LIM-6 a real marine-cad-ux-expert Adversary pass before build.

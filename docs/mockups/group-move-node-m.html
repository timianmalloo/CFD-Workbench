Group move and typed value

Proposal for node M (several points) · mockup for approval before any build · Example foil, trailing-edge rail

Checking…

1 · Drag a group to the tip limit: three ways to hold

Same gesture in each column. Points 9 and 10 of the trailing-edge rail are selected; point 10 is the tip vertex. The pointer grabs point 10 and drags toward the leading edge, 28 mm up. The rule is max(5 mm, 2 % of the root chord), so the limit is mm. The drawing is the outer 60 mm of the half-wing at 6 px per mm. Ruling 96 says a group that includes an end vertex holds the whole group. Column A does that. Column B holds only the limited point. Column C is what a group selection does today.

2 · Typed value for several points: set-all, move-by, or both

Three points selected on the trailing-edge rail: points 8, 9 and 10 (From root 390, 420 and 450 mm; aft 36, 33 and 30 mm). Both rows read Mixed because the values differ. The columns are the choice in DR-GM-2. A shared value is shown as a value, not as Mixed (spec: "shared values, Mixed where they differ").

3 · Typed refusals: nothing is rewritten, and the way out is one click

Same rule as a single point. The typed text stays and the field is marked invalid. Geometry and undo depth do not change. A Use action offers the nearest allowed value and is never applied by itself (Ruling 96).

4 · Keyboard: arrows move the whole selection, hold at the limit, one announcement

Shift+Up moves every selected point 1 mm toward the leading edge per press, as the Up arrow does on screen 1 (the channel ladder: 0.01 · 0.1 · 1 mm for Command on macOS or Ctrl on Windows, plain, Shift). Points 9 and 10 are selected; the tip starts at 8 mm. Presses past the limit do not bank steps, so one Down press moves the group back at once. The strip speaks on arrival at the limit, and again when a nudge frees the hold and a later one meets it. Key-up ends the run as one undo step.

Ten presses: six Up, one Down, three Up

Computed with the same rule as the other screens

Live region text, spoken at the arrival rows: “Tip chord is at its minimum, mm.” Focus stays on the focused point; the other selected points are not announced one by one.

5 · Hard states

Under the recommended choices. Each card is a state the build must render.

Choices for the operator

  1. DR-GM-1 which points can move together. A one curve at a time (recommended first slice) · B the two planform rails together, so the tip can be swept as one move · C any selection.
  2. DR-GM-2 typed value. A set-all only · B move-by only · C both, chosen per row, with Aft and value rows defaulting to Set to and From root fixed to Move by (recommended).
  3. DR-GM-3 how a group holds. A the whole group holds as one rigid body for every limit (recommended; Ruling 96 for the tip) · B only the limited point holds and the group deforms.
  4. DR-GM-4 press on a selected point. A drags the whole selection; a click without a drag collapses to that point on release (recommended) · B drags only the pressed point, as today.
  5. DR-GM-5 a point that cannot move on one axis. A the group holds on that axis and moves on the other (recommended) · B refuse the whole move · C leave that point behind.
  6. DR-GM-6 a handle whose anchor is not selected. A refuse the group move and say why (recommended) · B leave the handle out · C move it anyway.
  7. DR-GM-7 copy COPY-G1 to COPY-G12 as drawn here.
  8. DR-GM-8 spec retire the node M note and add the CAD-04 group clause.
  9. DR-GM-9 order. Build this after the limit increment, or take comb scale and monotone count first.
